# WORLD_2026_27.md — worlds, and the SWOS 96/97 Mod's World 2026-27 in OpenSWOS

**Status: plan (2026-10-26).** Nothing below is implemented. Written from a read of the Mod
(github.com/aminta/swos-9697-mod, code MIT since d44fa51), its author's proposal
(`aminta/OpenSWOS`, branch `convergence-docs`) and his `swos-competition-engine`, against our
code at 584367a8. Line numbers drift: search by name.

## 1. What we are doing, and on whose terms

Davide Lorigliola's **SWOS 96/97 Mod** patches the DOS game with whole new *career worlds*.
Its **World 2026-27** is today's football: 91 countries, 2,385 clubs, every league in its real
format (splits, play-offs and play-outs, Apertura/Clausura, promedio, Serie C/D groups), the
36-club league phases and their qualifying rounds, every confederation's club cups. We want it
in OpenSWOS as **the default world, with 96/97 (and our Copa Libertadores) one switch away**,
on **both targets**.

The decisions this plan rests on (the owner's, 2026-10-26):

- **We port the Mod's working code, not the proposed engine.** The Mod runs World 2026-27
  today with ~5,900 lines of freestanding C (`tools/gplay.c`, `dplay.c`, `uefaq.c`,
  `conti.c`, `statesim.c`) driven by tables its Python generators write. That C works on the
  original game's own structures (the DIY competition records, the team cache, 684-byte team
  records, the season end) — which are exactly ours, since OpenSWOS is a faithful port of the
  same 96/97 v1.52 edition. `swos-competition-engine` (a schema, a compiler and a binary
  format, no runtime yet) is not the base; its specs may become an *input* later (§9).
- **Our design, our tree.** The ported code lives in `src/`, called from our season-end
  code, typed against `include/game_types.h` — no hooks, no byte offsets, no DOS layouts.
  It keeps Davide's MIT notice (§8). If it ends up cleanly separable we offer it back.
- **The Amiga keeps up.** Everything is freestanding C with static, bounded state, read from
  baked C or binary files the setup makes; nothing parses JSON on the 68k.
- **The 96/97 world does not change.** Its season end runs the original code, untouched,
  and the replay/rollback/netplay rigs keep proving it.
- **Data licensing is set aside for now** (owner, 2026-10-26). The Mod's data is not MIT;
  Davide's private copies stay out of git until that is settled.
- **World 2026-27 is the default going forward** (owner, 2026-10-27), 96/97 one switch away.
  The player's own SWOS cannot produce it, so the data has to reach players with OpenSWOS (in
  the release, or fetched by the setup): the licence above must be settled before the first
  release that carries it. Nothing before that release waits on it.
- **The full world on the Amiga** (owner, 2026-10-27): the extra is fast RAM, which an A1200
  with 8 MB of fast RAM (the setup's stated minimum) has; nothing of it may touch chip RAM.

## 2. How the Mod does it (the reference)

A world is **one career pack per country** (`packs/`, `packs/CAREERS.md`), compiled by
`tools/mkseason.py` / `careerworld.py` into:

- **team files** — a country is a file number (Italy 2026-27 = 102), up to 256 clubs per file
  (the team word is `file | ordinal << 8`); clubs beyond the 16-player record in `XTW*`;
- **per-world country tables** (`countriesTable`, `competitionsTable`, `teamsCountryNumbers`)
  swapped in when a world's career starts (`w_ns_set`): every world reuses files 101-251;
  reserved numbers for the foreign-market pool (190), continental guests (191-194), the
  CLASSIC CAREERS menu (200+n);
- **the season-end engines**, at hooks in the original season end:

| module | lines | does | runs |
|---|---|---|---|
| `gplay.c` | 3,191 | league play-offs/play-outs/placement/title/continental play-offs from a byte spec; splits and stage tables; promedio; B teams; parallel groups by area (Serie C/D); a reserve pool below the lowest division; off-file pools | after each division's table (`gp_cap`), at the league's end (`gp_end`), in the new-season copy-back (`pg/rs/ri_season`) |
| `dplay.c` | 479 | Italy's Serie B/C play-offs and play-outs (hard-coded) | same |
| `uefaq.c` | 762 | the 2024-27 UEFA access list: qualifying rounds into three 36-club league phases, coefficients, ranking | season end, per country then once |
| `conti.c` | 1,006 | 12 other confederations' club cups (Libertadores, Sudamericana, CONCACAF, AFC, CAF, OFC, Intercontinental), 159 guest clubs | season end; the career club's rounds as "segments" |
| `statesim.c` | 310 | Brazil's 13 state championships | season end |

- **the 36-club league phase** (`lp36.py`): each European cup list carries 36 clubs, played as
  a type-2 contest of 2 groups of 18 with hooks for one calendar, one table and the split;
- **raised limits**: team-file buffer 64 KB → 176 KB (257 clubs), team cache 100 → 160,
  `someLeaguesTable` 2,000 → 2,409 entries, a 128-record pool;
- **state in the save**: a world byte, and named items in extra `.CAR` blocks (`EN`, `W1`, ...);
- **cosmetics**: purple kit (colour 10), face 3 (black skin, blond hair), shirt numbers 17-99
  in matches and menus, nationalities 153-213.

Every simulated tie uses the module's **own** LCG, seeded from the final tables, so the same
tables always give the same outcome: perfect for golden tests (§7). Only the career club's
matches are played; everything else is simulated by a strength model.

**Translations every port must make** (and never copy):

- His team word is little-endian `{file, ordinal}`; ours is `PACK_BE_U16(country, ordinal)`.
  Every `& 255` / `>> 8` is translated through the endian macros.
- His DIY offsets (0x31, 0x6D, 0x12D, 0x2B3) are our typed fields (`base.numTeams`,
  `matchData.league.teamEntryOffsets`, `.leagueTeamIds`, `leagueStandings[]`).
- **`teamEntryOffsets[14]` / `leagueTeamIds[14]`** are declared at the IDA label sizes, so a
  20-24 club league already writes past them into `competitionFormat` & co. They become a
  real typed array (≥ 32) *before* anything reads 32-row tables through them — an untangle in
  the sense of the roadmap's step 3.
- **His balance-byte hack** (a byte at `someLeaguesTable[1844]` that keeps the sum check
  quiet when moves cross divisions) is not ported: the moves stay paired per country, and the
  sum check stays a panic (`.claude/CLAUDE.md`, "Panics are signals").
- **64-bit arithmetic** (`conti.c` `match()` divides, `statesim.c` multiplies): the Amiga
  link has no libgcc. Rewrite in 32-bit, or provide the helpers.
- Their own `memset`/`memcpy` go; ours stay.
- Their per-module LCGs stay as they are (bit-exact with the Mod, and never our
  `Rand`/`Rand2`, which replays and netplay depend on). lp36's in-match draw uses the game's
  `Rand2`, as the Mod does.

## 4. Worlds in OpenSWOS

A **world** is a data set: countries, team files, competitions, the season-end rules.

- **Identity.** A short tag (`9697`, `2627`) and a data version. Every save writes
  `"world"`; a save without one is 9697. A mismatched world or data version refuses the load
  with a reason, as a newer save does today.
- **Source in the repo, built into the user's folder** (owner, 2026-10-27). The world's data
  lives in the repo like our other data (`bin/data_json/worlds/<tag>/`: teams, packs,
  competitions) and ships with OpenSWOS, as the Copa Libertadores does; OpenSWOS Setup
  generates the game's files from it into the assets folder (below), for SDL and the Amiga.
  Its team **uuids and checksums are assigned in the repo**, so they are canonical: the
  server's registry and the netplay team check use them. Until Davide agrees to publish,
  `bin/data_json/worlds/` stays ignored (`.gitignore`): it lives in the working tree only.
- **Files.** `assets/worlds/<tag>/` in the assets folder: `data/TEAM.nnn` (RNC, as today),
  the pool, the extra-players files, and **one portable big-endian file of the world's
  tables** — countries, competitions, continent/country lists, rule specs, strings. SDL may
  read the world's JSON directly; the Amiga reads only the binaries. 9697 is `worlds/9697`,
  made from the player's disks as now (`bin/data` stays where it is until then).
- **The setup** (`tools/swosdata`) gets a world step: a pack → `TEAM.nnn` + the tables file,
  through the writer `scripts/emit_teams.py` already proves byte-exact. The Mod's generators
  are ported to write this, not his exe patches. Their validators come too.
- **Country numbers.** Per world, as the Mod does: a world swaps its country tables in when
  a career, a season or a team picker opens it. The 96/97 numbers are not renumbered.
- **Team ids.** `uuid5(NS, "<tag>/team/<file>/<ordinal>")`; 96/97 keeps its ids. The
  checksum (positions, skills, values) as today. The server's registry gets a world column
  (`assign_team_uuids.py --registry` per world; `SWTC` carries the tag), and the lobby
  compares world + team.
- **Netplay.** The world is part of what the handshake compares. A world change is a
  `NETPLAY_COMPAT` raise if it changes how a match plays (`include/netplay_compat.h`).
- **Choosing.** A setting (`[general] world`, default 2627 once it ships) and a picker where
  careers, seasons and friendlies choose teams. Country lists page, so the Mod's 44-entry
  limit does not apply.

Every raise is its own change, built on both targets, with the 96/97 rigs passing. Where a
structure is saved (the cache, the leagues table, the competition copies), the save keeps
named fields of variable length, never a bigger raw block.

**Amiga memory** (estimate, only the active world loaded): team-file buffer +111 KB, cache and
its season-end copy ≈ +130 KB, tables 150-250 KB, pools ≈ 250 KB, the modules' static state
≈ 150 KB (`conti.c` 90 KB BSS can shrink). About **+0.6-0.8 MB of fast RAM** — the full world
ships on the Amiga (§1). None of it chip: every new buffer is checked in `bin/SWOS2.map`; the
WHDLoad slave's sizes follow from the hunks.

## 6. Phases

Each phase ends with both targets building, the 96/97 rigs green, and its own tests.

0. **Golden tests of today's game** (also the Mod's proposal's phase 0). **Done 2026-10-27**
   on SDL: `scripts/career_golden_test.sh`. The game's career autopilot
   (`--career-golden`, `src/career_autopilot.c`) plays new careers picked by label
   (`tests/career_golden_careers.txt`: England's Premier League and Division One, Italy,
   Germany, Spain, Scotland, Brazil) and any local saves (`tests/career`) for five seasons,
   writing the game's own JSON save at every season end; `tests/career_golden.txt` holds
   their hashes. Everything after this must leave them identical, or be a deliberate
   change recorded with `--update`. Not yet: national-team careers (their screens are not
   driven), the FS-UAE harness. Making it repeatable found two save bugs (fixed): a stale
   address in the competition blobs (`namePtr`, and the stage snapshot's copy, which a
   loaded European-cup career put live), and the game tick the original reads for the
   chairman's decisions running on the wall clock.
1. **Worlds, with one world.** **Done 2026-10-27:** `include/world.h` (96/97 is world 0,
   so a byte that held 0 before worlds already means 96/97); `[general] world` (SDL; the
   Amiga plays 96/97); `"world"` in career and competition saves, a save without it is
   96/97 and one of another world is refused; the handshake compares it in the setup's
   spare byte (an older build sends 0 = 96/97, so no protocol change and no
   NETPLAY_COMPAT raise) with "YOU PLAY DIFFERENT WORLDS"; the matchmaker tag is
   `OPENSWOS/<n>` for 96/97 and `OPENSWOS/<n>-<world>` otherwise (the server's PlayKey
   already keeps them apart: no server change). The golden test's saves changed only by
   the new field. **Moved to phase 3**, where there is a second world to point them at:
   the per-world asset folder, country tables and team-file path, and the uuid
   namespace (96/97 keeps `96-97/team/...`; the registry needs no world column, since
   uuids are unique across worlds).
2. **Typed tables.** The `[14]` league arrays → real arrays; the team cache, leagues table
   and team-file buffer sized per world; `divisions[4]` → per world. 9697 still identical.
   **Measured 2026-10-27** against Davide's packs (what the world really needs, not the
   table in §5's guesses):
   - **The `[14]` arrays need no memory moved.** `teamEntryOffsets` and `leagueTeamIds`
     sit in the match-data union, and a league's entries past 14 run into cup-only scratch
     (`cupRoundResults`, then `cupAggregateScores` from 0x14E): a 24-club 96/97 league
     already does, as the original did, and the save writes them by `numTeams`. Even 32
     clubs end at 0xAE and 0x16E, inside that scratch. Only the declared sizes lie.
   - **Clubs per division**: 24 is enough except MLS and Argentina (30), both played as
     two zones / conferences of 15; the fixture tables (`_fixtureScheduleNTeams`, 2..24)
     stay, unless a 30-club single table is wanted.
   - **Divisions per league**: `divisions[4]` is enough for 89 of 91 countries; Italy has 14
     (its Serie C and D groups are divisions of one league, as in the Mod) and El Salvador 5.
   - **Clubs per team file**: 93 is enough for 96 of 98 files; Italy has 256 and Brazil's
     second file 119.
   - **Competitions**: 205 in the world, against 128 free ids -- ids are per world (phase 3).
3. **A first 26/27 world within today's limits**: Italy's Serie A and B and one Serie C group
   (60 clubs in one file), the Coppa Italia, no Europe. The setup builds it from a pack; a
   two-season career on SDL and the Amiga.
4. **League rules**: port `gplay.c` + `dplay.c` → `src/league_playoffs.c`,
   `league_playoffs_ita.c` (§3); their spec generator → typed JSON
   (`bin/data_json/competitions/league_rules.json` per world) baked by
   `scripts/emit_competitions.py` for the Amiga. Serie B/C play-offs, play-outs, then the
   whole Italian pyramid (256 clubs: phase 2's limits).
5. **Europe**: port `uefaq.c` and the 36-club league phase; the count gate becomes per world;
   the qualifiers consume the engine's lists. 9697 keeps 16/32/32.
6. **Other confederations**: `conti.c`, `statesim.c`, the guest clubs.
7. **Cosmetics**: purple, face 3, shirt numbers to 99, nationalities to 213.
8. **The whole world**, and **2627 the default** (with the data licence settled).

## 7. Testing

- **Phase 0's golden career** guards 96/97 throughout.
- **Bit-exact against the Mod.** His modules seed their LCGs from the final tables, so
  feeding the same tables to his C (built natively, unchanged) and to our port must give the
  same moves, draws and entrants. A small native harness per module (`--worlds-selftest`)
  compares the two; his C stays out of the binaries, in the test build only.
- **His harness scenarios as cases.** `tools/swostest.py` states a checkable invariant per
  scenario — e.g. `world_playoffs` (Italy: every division holds 20, B sends 3 up / 4 down),
  `world_dplay_b/_out/_c`, `world_groups`, `world_split_*`, `world_gplay*`, `world2526q`,
  `lp36_tourney`, `world_conti_guest`, `world_statesim`. Each becomes a forced-result case in
  our career test.
- **Saves**: a world career saved and reloaded every season, across SDL and the Amiga.
